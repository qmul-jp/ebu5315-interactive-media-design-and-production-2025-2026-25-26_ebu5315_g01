Basic

The following 8 theorems belong to the basic theorems

Basic Theorem 1

Angle at the Centre

The angle subtended at the centre of a circle is twice the angle subtended at any point on the circumference, from the same arc.

If two chords from the same arc meet at the centre and at the rim, the central angle is the larger one. This links inscribed angles to arc geometry and is a foundation for many circle proofs.

Basic Theorem 2

Angles in a Semicircle

Any angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle (90°).

The diameter is the chord across the flat side of the semicircle. This is the special case of the angle-at-centre rule when the central angle is 180°.

Basic Theorem 3

Cyclic Quadrilateral

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.

A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on one circle. Opposite angles are supplementary, which often unlocks angle-chasing in harder diagrams.

! Converse idea: If one pair of opposite angles adds to 180°, the four points may be concyclic.
Basic Theorem 4

Radius to a Tangent

A radius drawn to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent: the angle between them is 90°.

A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point. The radius to that touching point meets the tangent at a right angle, which is key in construction and coordinate problems.

! Pair with: Combine with equal tangent lengths from an external point; many questions use both facts together.
Basic Theorem 5

Angles in the Same Segment

Angles subtended by the same chord (or arc) at the circumference, on the same side of the chord, are equal.

Treat the chord as dividing the circle into two arcs. Any point on the same arc sees the same chord under the same angle; switching arc gives the supplementary angle pair.

! Label carefully: Same segment means same side of the chord; do not mix angles from opposite arcs.
Basic Theorem 6

Tangents from a Point to a Circle

From an external point, the two tangent segments to a circle have equal lengths.

The two tangents form a symmetric V-shape from the external point. Equal tangent lengths often simplify perimeter and length equations.

! Construction: Mark the two right angles where radii meet tangents; then combine with Pythagoras when lengths are given.
Basic Theorem 7

Tangents from a Point (II)

The line from the external point to the centre bisects the angle between the two tangents, and bisects the angle between the two radii to the contact points.

Symmetry about the line through the external point and centre creates matched angles and distances. This is useful in loci and structured geometry proofs.

Basic Theorem 8

Alternate Segment Theorem

The angle between a tangent and a chord through the contact point equals the angle in the alternate segment, subtended by the chord in the opposite arc.

This links tangent direction to an inscribed angle on the opposite arc. It appears frequently in tangent-chord angle-chasing questions.

! Which segment? The alternate segment is the arc opposite the tangent-chord angle, not the arc containing that angle.
